My basement has water and the pump is not running

That is the call we hear most when it rains hard. The pump may be unplugged, jammed, tripped on a breaker, stuck on a float or simply worn out. We check the power, the float, the check valve and the discharge line in that order, and then decide whether the fix is a repair or a new pump.

  • ✓Pump hums but moves no water
  • ✓Pump never turns on, even when the pit is full
  • ✓Pump runs constantly or cycles every few seconds
  • ✓Water in the pit but none in the discharge pipe
  • ✓Loud rattling or grinding while it runs

Flooding basement: steps while you wait

Before the technician arrives

Stay clear of electric

If water covers outlets or the panel, do not step in. Call us and your electric utility if the panel is wet.

Check the plug and breaker

If the pump is on a GFCI outlet, press reset. If it trips again, stop and tell us.

Lift items off the floor

Move boxes and electronics onto shelves or upstairs. Photograph the water line on walls and belongings for your insurer.

Look at the discharge

Make sure the outdoor end is not frozen, buried or clogged. Do not reach into the pit.

Why Winslow basements see water after storms

The state describes the Township's water source as a Kirkwood-Cohansey water-table aquifer system, which means the ground water level follows rainfall instead of staying steady.

In a wet season, the water table can rise toward a basement floor and push water into the pit. That is why a pump can run for days after a storm, and why a backup plan matters.

Winslow's Levitt-built Sicklerville neighborhoods are more than fifty years old, and a finished basement is not part of every house there. We cannot say which houses have pits. If your house has one, test it before storm season, and if it does not and you take on water, ask us what it would take.

Sump pump installation and replacement

Replacing a pump means pulling the old unit, checking the pit for silt, fitting a check valve so water does not fall back, and testing the float with a bucket of water. Installing a new pit is bigger work, since it involves cutting the slab and routing a discharge line out of the house.

Where the water goes matters. Discharge lines should send water away from the foundation and not toward a neighbor's yard or into the sanitary sewer. Plumbing connections follow the state plumbing subcode, the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, and permit rules vary, so ask the Township Construction Office.

Pump repair versus swapping the whole unit

We repair a pump when the cause is simple, such as a stuck float, a bad switch or a clogged check valve. We replace it when the motor is worn, the housing is cracked or the unit is old enough that another failure is likely. We will explain which one applies and give the price first.

Ejector pump for a basement bath

An ejector pump is a different machine. It collects wastewater from a below-grade toilet, shower or sink and lifts it up to the house drain. It sits in a sealed basin and often has a vent.

If it fails, you may notice slow drains, a gurgle or a smell of sewage in the basement, and we treat that as an urgent call.

Basement waterproofing: plumber or waterproofing contractor?

Waterproofing means keeping water out of a basement through the walls, floor or footing. Interior drain systems, wall sealing and exterior excavation are done by waterproofing contractors, not by plumbers. A sump pump is the piece that moves collected water away, and that part is plumbing.

So the honest split is this. If the water enters through the wall or floor joint and the pump is fine, you may need a waterproofing contractor. If the pump is old, wet, jammed, or the discharge is blocked, call a plumber. If you cannot tell which you have, we will look and tell you.
