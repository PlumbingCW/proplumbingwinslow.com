Winter: the pipe that froze while you slept

A hard freeze is the classic night call. Pipes in an unheated crawl space, a garage wall or a basement corner freeze first, and the split often shows up only when the ice melts and water starts to flow. If a faucet runs weak in January, treat it as a possible freeze before you treat it as a pressure problem.

Shut off the water at the main, open the faucet nearest the frozen pipe, and do not use a flame to thaw it.

Once pipe has cracked, burst pipe repair means cutting out the damaged section and replacing it, then checking the rest of the run for a second split. Call dispatch first so someone is heading your way while you shut things off.

Water is already spraying

Close the main shutoff, then switch off power to any wet room from a dry spot. If you cannot reach a dry breaker, leave it alone and call. Never stand in water near a panel or outlet.

Spring: wet basements and the sump pump

Heavy rain finds the lowest point in a house. A basement that stays dry in August may take on water in April, and a sump pump that sat idle all winter has to start without warning. Test yours by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. The float should lift and the pump should empty the pit in seconds.

If the pump hums but does not move water, or it runs constantly, it may be clogged, stuck or undersized. Sump pump repair or replacement fixes that, but a pump cannot fix a foundation crack or gutters that dump beside the wall. A waterproofing contractor handles those, and we will say so plainly.

Summer: outdoor faucets and the underground line

Summer water use hides leaks. A line from the meter to the house can leak in the yard for weeks, and the only clues are a high bill, a soft spot in the lawn or low pressure.

Turn off every fixture and watch the meter. If it keeps moving, water line repair may be the answer, and that is a dig, so we locate the leak first.

Outdoor spigots are the other summer problem. A hose left attached through a freeze can crack the pipe inside the wall, and the leak only appears once you turn the water on in May. Check inside the wall below the spigot.

Fall: get ahead of heating season

Before the cold arrives, walk your basement. Look for green crust on fittings, drips under supply valves and a water heater that rumbles. Fixing a drip in October is far less disruptive than a burst in January. We do not diagnose boilers on this site, but we can tell you what to ask a heating contractor.

Utilities, permits and the Atco ZIP puzzle

The Township has a Utilities Department and a Construction and Zoning office at the municipal building, 2131 Auburn Avenue in Atco, 856-768-2300, weekdays 8:30 to 4:30. We have not confirmed which utility serves every street, so ask the Township Utilities Department. The Construction Office decides whether a job needs a permit, because permit rules vary by job.

Atco's ZIP, 08004, crosses a township line. West Atco belongs to Winslow, so a burst pipe at a West Atco address means Winslow's Construction Office, not Waterford's. We sort that out when you call. Neighbors across the line in Berlin Borough and in Hammonton call the same dispatch for burst pipe and sump pump trouble.
