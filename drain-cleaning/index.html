Emergency signs a clogged drain cannot wait

Some slow drains can wait for a weekday visit. Others cannot, and the difference is usually where the water goes. If it spills over a rim, rises in a second fixture, or smells like sewage, treat it as urgent.

  • ✓Water backs up in the tub or shower when the washer drains
  • ✓A toilet gurgles when a sink empties
  • ✓Water pools around a basement floor drain
  • ✓A kitchen sink fills and will not go down at all
  • ✓More than one fixture is slow at the same time

Two or more fixtures acting up together usually point to a shared line, not a single trap. That is the moment to stop using water and call. A same-day appointment, when available, keeps a backed-up line from soaking flooring and cabinets.

We start with the lightest tool that will actually solve the problem. If a cable only punches a hole through the clog and the drain slows again within weeks, the next step is usually a high-pressure water jet to scour the pipe wall.

How snaking and cabling works

A drain snake is a flexible steel cable on a motorized drum. The plumber feeds it into the drain through a cleanout or fixture opening until it meets the blockage, then turns the cutting head to break it up or hook it. The cable is pulled back out and the line is flushed to confirm it runs.

1

Find the access point

We locate a cleanout, remove a trap, or pull a toilet if that is the only way in. Where we enter depends on where the clog is.

2

Match the cable to the pipe

A thin cable suits a bathroom sink. A heavier cable with a larger head is used for a 3 or 4 inch line.

3

Clear and retrieve

The head cuts through the blockage and pulls debris back where it can be removed instead of pushed further down.

4

Flush and test

We run water for several minutes at the fixture and any connected ones, and check for leaks at the opened joints.

A cable can scratch an old pipe or snag on a broken joint. If it hangs up at the same spot repeatedly, we stop forcing it and recommend a camera look before anything else is pushed down the line.

Kitchen sink clogs, the usual suspects

A kitchen sink drain carries cooking grease, soap film and food scraps, and those stick to each other as the water cools in the pipe. The slowdown is gradual until one day the sink stops draining and may overflow into the second basin or the dishwasher.

Hot water and dish soap rarely fix a true grease clog because the grease only moves a few feet and sets again. We clear the line with a cable or jet, then explain what to keep out of the drain.

If the sink is tied to a disposal, we check that unit too, since a jammed disposal and a clogged line look alike from above.

Slow showers and tubs

Hair and soap are the main cause of shower and tub clogs, and the blockage often sits in the trap just below the drain opening. Many are cleared with a short cable, without removing anything. If water rises in the tub when another fixture drains, the clog is farther down and the main line is involved.

Drain line leaks look like clogs

A cracked or separated drain line can leak while still carrying water, so the symptoms get mixed up. Stains on a ceiling below a bathroom, a damp spot on a basement wall, or a musty smell with no visible water can all trace back to a drain line, not a supply line.

We do not guess. We run water through the suspect fixture, look at the joints and, where needed, send a camera. A line that is leaking cannot be fixed by cleaning it, so you hear that before any cleaning is done.

Why drains clog in Winslow homes

Thousands of homes in Sicklerville were built in the 1970s, including the Winslow Crossing streets of Primrose Gate, Manor Hall and Arbor Meadows.

Houses that age have had decades of grease, soap scale and tree roots working on their drain lines. If your house is on a 08081 street and clogs keep returning, ask for a camera inspection instead of another quick clear.

Winslow is also unusual in that about 80 percent of the township sits inside the Pinelands National Reserve. Many lots are wooded, and roots seek out moisture in joints. For a repeat clog in a yard-facing line in 08095 or 08018, roots are one of the first things we check.

Whether you are on sewer or septic depends on your street. Township sewer customers connect through the Department of Municipal Utilities and the Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority.

Call the DMU at 609-567-0700 if you are unsure which side of the cleanout the utility handles. We clear the house drains in either case, and a septic contractor handles tanks and fields.

Stop the water

Do not flush, run the washer or use the dishwasher. More water has nowhere to go.

Find the cleanout
