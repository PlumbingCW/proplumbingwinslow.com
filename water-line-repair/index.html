Emergency Water Line Repair in Winslow, NJ

Your water line, also called the service line, is the pipe that carries water from the utility connection at the street or meter to your house. If it is leaking, you may see a soaked yard, a meter that spins with everything off, or pressure that sags. Why is my yard wet and my pressure low?

A soggy strip of lawn, a dip in the driveway or a faucet that now dribbles are the signs most people notice first. They often come from the same cause, a break in the buried line that feeds the house. The technical name is a water service line leak, and finding it comes before any digging.

Where your responsibility starts and the utility's ends

In general, the utility owns the main in the street and the connection to it, and the homeowner owns the pipe from there to the house. Ownership can differ by street and by utility, so confirm yours. Winslow Township's Dept. of Municipal Utilities can be reached at 609-567-0700 on weekdays, 8:30 to 4:30.

The Township's system buys water through an interconnection with New Jersey American Water, in addition to drawing from its own wells. Which supplier serves your street does not change who fixes a break on your side of the line, but it does decide who you call about the meter. We do not work on utility-owned equipment.

Water service line leak: how we find and fix it

1

Confirm it is the service line

We close the house valve and watch the meter. Motion with the house valve closed puts the leak outside.

2

Locate the break

We use pressure testing and listening gear to narrow the leak to a short stretch before any digging.

3

Choose the repair

A short cut-in repair, a longer replacement run or a trenchless route if the layout allows it.

4

Restore and test

We test the new section under pressure, then close the trench, and flag permit steps with the Construction Office.

Trenchless replacement pulls or bursts a new pipe through the old path with fewer holes. It does not suit every property, so we tell you when open trenching is the better choice. Where the pipe is old across its whole length, a full run may make more sense than another spot patch.

Low water pressure: a leak, a valve or something else

Low pressure is not always a line leak. A partly closed valve, a clogged aerator or a failing pressure regulator can all cause it. If only one faucet is weak, it is likely local to that fixture. If every fixture drops at once, we look at the main valve, the regulator and the service line.

A sudden drop with a wet yard points to a break. A slow decline over months suggests corrosion or mineral buildup inside older pipe. We do not state a hardness level for the Township supply, and we suggest testing your own tap if scale is a worry.

Meter and curb box notes

The curb box is the small lid near the street that covers the shutoff valve. If it is buried, cracked or crowded with leaves, finding the valve in an emergency wastes time. Photograph it now and clear it, but do not turn a utility valve unless the utility tells you to.

A meter box full of water is a signal worth reporting. The seep may be at the meter or just past it, and we can tell the difference with a pressure test. If the meter or its connections are the cause, the DMU takes it from there.

What Winslow's ground and housing mean for water lines

Winslow Township sits in the Pinelands region, with about 80 percent inside the Pinelands National Reserve. The aquifer under it is described by the state as a water-table system that follows rainfall. After a wet week a lawn can stay soft for reasons that have nothing to do with the pipe, so we test before we dig.

Sicklerville's 1970s Levitt-built neighborhoods now have service lines that are fifty years or more old. That does not mean a line is bad. It does mean that a lawn patch in 08081 that stays wet in dry weather deserves a pressure test, and the same goes for older homes in 08095, 08018, 08004 and 08037.

Pressure testing, then repair or replace

Many service line jobs end in a choice between repairing a short section and replacing the run. Repair is quicker when the pipe is sound everywhere else. Replacement is better when we find several weak spots, because each fix leaves older pipe on both sides under stress.

When the damage is wider than one pipe, such as a fixture line inside the house, our leak detection work covers the interior search. For a house-wide pipe replacement, see our repiping page. Nearby towns we serve, like Berlin Borough, are listed on our Berlin page.
