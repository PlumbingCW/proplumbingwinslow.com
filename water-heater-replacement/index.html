Flooding from a failed tank: what to do before we arrive

A tank that has rusted through does not usually stop on its own. The water keeps coming until the supply is closed, so the shutoff on the cold line is the single most useful thing you can touch. It is normally a lever or round handle on the pipe entering the top of the heater.

  • ✓Close the cold water valve above the heater. If it will not turn, close the main shutoff for the house.
  • ✓Turn the breaker off for an electric heater, or set the gas control to off for a gas one.
  • ✓Do not step into standing water near outlets or a panel. Cut power to that area first if you can reach the breaker safely.
  • ✓Move stored boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor, then take photos for your insurance claim.

Gas smell or a flooded panel

With a gas smell, get everyone outside and phone the gas utility or 911 from there. If water has reached the electrical panel, stay away and call 911. A plumber cannot work in either situation until it is made safe.

Is the tank really finished? Signs that point to replacement

Not every leaking heater needs to be replaced. A drip at a fitting or a relief valve is a repair, and we say so. Water from the bottom of the tank, a seam on the jacket or a rust-colored puddle usually means the steel tank has failed, and nothing fits over that.

Other signs add up over time. Repeated repairs on the same unit, rusty hot water at every tap, loud popping from sediment, and a unit that cannot keep up anymore all push the decision toward a new heater. If you are on the edge, a straight repair assessment will separate a part failure from a tank failure.

Gas, electric or heat pump: picking the new heater

Most Winslow replacements stay with the same fuel, because switching changes the venting, the gas line or the electrical circuit. A swap to the same fuel and a similar size is the simplest job. A change in fuel, location or capacity is a bigger project and belongs on the planned side of the ledger.

A heat pump water heater moves heat from the surrounding air into the tank instead of making heat directly. The U.S. Department of Energy says it can be two to three times more energy efficient than a conventional electric resistance heater.

It needs a space that stays between 40 and 90 degrees Fahrenheit year-round and at least 1,000 cubic feet of air around it, and it cools the room it sits in.

That space rule matters in practice. A heat pump unit fits a roomy basement or garage better than a small closet in a Sicklerville 08081 townhouse.

We look at where the old heater sits and tell you honestly whether a heat pump model is a fit. For homeowners who want to weigh a tankless unit instead, the tankless page covers that.

Code, permits and what the Construction Office decides

The state plumbing code is the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, adopted at N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. A master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor or their W-2 employees must do the work. License numbers have to appear on vehicles and advertising, so ask for one and look it up with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers at njconsumeraffairs.gov/plu.

Whether a swap needs a permit is the Township's decision, not ours. The Construction Office sits in the Department of Community Services at 609-567-0700 option 5, and plumbing inspections run Tuesday to Thursday mornings. Permit rules vary, so a call before a bigger change saves delay.

In practice, a replacement also brings the surrounding parts up for review: the relief valve and its discharge line, the shutoff valves, the flex connectors and, in some homes, an expansion tank. We cover that in more depth on the planned installation page, since those choices matter most when the job is not an emergency.
