Garbage disposal repair means diagnosing the unit, its drain connection and its power, then fixing what failed or replacing the disposal when it is worn out. Most calls come down to one of four things: a jam, a leak, a clog past the unit, or a motor that has quit. The plumber tells you which, and what each choice costs, before the work starts.

What the sound or the puddle is telling you

  • ✓A hum with no spinning means the blades are jammed, and the motor is straining.
  • ✓Silence and no hum often means a tripped reset button or a dead outlet.
  • ✓Water beneath the sink during use points to a seal, a drain gasket or a split in the housing.
  • ✓Water in the second basin means a blockage downstream, not always the disposal.
  • ✓A grinding sound with no food in it suggests a foreign object or a damaged flywheel.

A hum is the most common call and the least urgent, as long as power is switched off. A leak from the body of the unit is a different story. If the housing itself has a crack or rust hole, it cannot be sealed, and the disposal gets replaced.

Leaking under the sink right now

Leaks come from three places. The top flange where the sink meets the disposal drips when its mounting is loose or the putty has dried.

The side outlet to the drain trap drips when its gasket fails, and the dishwasher inlet drips when its hose clamp is loose. A leak from the underside, or one that appears only when the unit runs, usually means the seal inside is worn out.

Dry the cabinet, run a small amount of water, and watch which spot forms the first drop. That detail, shared on the phone, helps dispatch plan the visit. Do not run the disposal again until it is checked, since a steady drip near a motor is both a water and an electrical concern.

Disposals are not built to last forever, and a unit with a failing motor and a leaking seal is telling you to stop spending on it. The plumber can give you both numbers, so the choice is yours. Horsepower, quiet-run models and a continuous-feed versus batch design all affect what you pay.

The dishwasher connection

The dishwasher drain hose connects to a small port on the side of the disposal. The port comes with a plug inside that must be knocked out when a dishwasher is hooked up.

If a new disposal goes in and the plug is left in place, the dishwasher cannot drain, and you find a pool in the bottom of the machine.

If a dishwasher is backing up into the sink, the cause can be a clogged port, a kinked hose, or a high loop that has dropped. Plumbers check the connection every time a disposal is replaced. When the whole drain behind the sink is slow, it points to drain cleaning rather than a bad unit.
