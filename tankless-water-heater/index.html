Tankless Water Heater in Winslow, NJ

A tankless water heater in Winslow, NJ is a wall-mounted unit that heats water only when a tap is open, so there is no tank of stored hot water to run out. We install new units, flush and descale existing ones, and help sort out units that shut off, throw error codes or give lukewarm water.

Hot water runs cold halfway through, or the heater keeps shutting off?

Lukewarm showers, water that surges hot and cold, and a unit that cuts out when two taps are open are common complaints with on-demand heaters. They usually point to a tankless water heater that is scaled up, undersized for the demand or showing a fault. The fix is a flush, a setting change, a part or sometimes a different unit.

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

Flushing means running a descaling solution through the heat exchanger with a small pump, using the isolation valves most tankless units are fitted with, and then rinsing it clear. Minerals in the water bake onto the hot surfaces. Over time they narrow the passages, so the unit has to work harder and may fault out.

How often a flush is needed depends on the water, the unit and how it is used, and the manufacturer's manual is the right guide. We follow it. If your heater has no service valves, we can add them during the visit, which makes every later flush simpler.

  • ✓Gradual loss of hot water volume or temperature over months
  • ✓Error codes that clear after a restart and then return
  • ✓Rumbling or kettle-like noise from the unit
  • ✓Longer waits for hot water at the farthest faucet
  • ✓A flush that has never been done on a unit several years old

Test your tap

The Township's water quality report carries no hardness figure, so we will not guess one. A home test kit or a lab test of your tap shows what your heater really sees. The DMU at 609-567-0700 can also send the annual report.

What tankless means for a Winslow home

A tankless unit gives a steady supply while a tap is running, which suits households that run back-to-back showers or fill a large tub. It does not suit every house. Several fixtures at once can exceed what one unit can heat, and a unit sized for a small family can struggle in a big one.

Because the gas or electric requirements are usually larger, a conversion from a tank can mean a new gas line size, a new vent route or a panel upgrade. We assess that on site and put it in one price. For the broader planning side of any new heater, see our installation page.

Installing a new unit or servicing the one you have

Installation and service are different visits. Installation starts with the load: which fixtures run together, the gas or electric supply, the vent path and where the unit will hang. Service starts with the symptom, the model and the error code, and may end in a flush, a part or a recommendation to replace.

Not every brand and model is something we repair, and we say so up front rather than send a truck for nothing. If a repair would cost close to a new unit, we tell you that too. When a unit has failed entirely, a repair assessment will show whether a part or a replacement makes more sense.

Code, permits and the licensed-plumber requirement

New Jersey's plumbing subcode is the 2021 edition of the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, at N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Work belongs to a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor or their W-2 employees, and the license number has to show on vehicles and advertising. Verify it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers at njconsumeraffairs.gov/plu.

Whether a tankless installation needs a permit is for the Winslow Construction Office to decide. Reach it on 609-567-0700 option 5, with plumbing inspections Tuesday to Thursday from 8am to 1pm. Permit rules vary with the scope, so ask before you order a unit.
