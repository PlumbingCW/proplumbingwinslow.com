Flooding, backups and leaking pipes in a commercial building

Business calls tend to fall into three groups. Water arriving where it should not, such as a ceiling leak or a burst line. Waste going the wrong direction, such as a floor drain or restroom backing up. And a fixture or system that stops working, like a water heater, a sink line or a grease interceptor.

For the first two, stop the source if you can, protect electrical equipment and stock, and tell the plumber where the main and any zone valves are.

For a sewage backup, close the restrooms, keep people out of the area, and do not run fixtures that feed the same line. See burst pipe repair if the main concern is a split pipe.

If you are a tenant, read the lease before calling. Many leases split responsibility, with the tenant covering fixtures inside the suite and the landlord covering mains, risers and the roof. A plumber can find the source and document it, and the lease determines who pays.

Commercial drains, floor drains and grease traps

Commercial drains carry more volume and more of whatever the business produces. Restaurants send fats and food solids, salons send hair and product, and shops with mop sinks send grit. A drain that slows down after every busy weekend is telling you that the line has built up, and cleaning it before it stops saves a closed day.

Floor drains matter because they are the first place a main line backup shows. A dry floor drain can also let sewer gas into a space, since the trap seal evaporates.

Grease traps and interceptors need regular cleaning on a schedule set by use and by local rules. Ask the Township's Department of Municipal Utilities what applies to your kind of business. For heavy build-up, hydro jetting clears the line more thoroughly than a cable.

Commercial leak detection, honestly scoped

If a water meter keeps moving after fixtures are off, or a bill jumps, a leak may be hiding in a wall, under a slab or in a buried line.

Finding it uses listening equipment, pressure testing and sometimes cameras, and the right method depends on the building. A one-story strip mall and a multi-floor office have very little in common.

The scope also depends on your lease and the pipe layout. A leak in the tenant's suite may be the tenant's to track, while a leak in a shared riser is the landlord's.

We tell you what can be found, what cannot be promised, and where another trade, such as a roofer or restoration crew, has to take over. The Township notes that a higher water bill can mean an undetected leak, so billing changes are worth acting on.

Backflow prevention and testing for businesses

Many commercial buildings have backflow preventers on irrigation, fire lines, boilers or kitchen equipment. Their job is to stop used water from flowing back into the clean supply.

Who must test them, and how often, depends on the device and local and state rules, so ask the Township's utilities department or Construction Office, and have the device model and location on hand when you book.
