Hydro jetting in Winslow, NJ is a drain cleaning method that uses a high-pressure water stream from a hose and nozzle to scour grease, scale and roots off the inside of a pipe. It cleans the whole pipe wall, not just a hole through the middle.

My snake got stuck, or the drain clogs again a few weeks later

That is the situation most people describe when they need a jet. A cable gets partway down the line and stops, or it opens the drain for a short while before the same slow flow returns. The cause is usually buildup on the pipe wall, or roots that grow back as soon as the cable pulls out.

The technical fix is hydro jetting. Water at high pressure cuts through grease and scale and flushes roots out of the line, then the debris is carried down to the sewer or washed back to a cleanout where it can be caught.

Snake or jet: how to decide

Cable machine compared with hydro jetting
QuestionCable machineHydro jetting
What does it do to the clog?Cuts or hooks it and pulls it outScours the pipe wall and flushes debris downstream
Good for roots?Cuts the roots it touches, which often regrowClears more of the root mass, but roots can still return
Good for grease and scale?Leaves a coating behindStrips it off the wall
Risk to old or broken pipe?Can snag on a jointNeeds a camera check first on cracked or collapsed pipe
Typical useFirst pass on a simple stoppageRepeat clogs and heavy buildup

A jet is not always the better answer. If the clog is a single toy or wipe lodged near a fixture, a cable is quicker and does less. We choose the tool after hearing where and how often the line backs up.

Backup and flooding signs that call for a jet

  • ✓Sewage or gray water comes up through a basement floor drain
  • ✓A cable stopped at the same distance every time it was tried
  • ✓Toilets gurgle and tubs slow after heavy water use
  • ✓A yard or driveway line clears and then clogs again
  • ✓Roots have been pulled out on a previous visit

If water is rising toward living space, call right away. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and after-hours calls go through the same 24/7 dispatch line.

How the jetting visit runs

1

Look before blasting

A camera goes in first when the pipe is old or the cause is unclear. We need to know the line is intact.

2

Open the access point

We use a cleanout, or pull a fixture, to get the hose into the line.

3

Jet the line in passes

The nozzle pulls itself forward along the pipe while the pressure scours the wall. Flow is checked at the end.

4

Verify with a second look

A camera pass confirms the pipe is clean and shows any cracks or offset joints that need repair.

When we will not jet

We do not run a jet through pipe that a camera shows is collapsed, badly cracked or separated. Pressure there can make the damage worse and push water into the soil. In that case the honest answer is a repair, and we explain it before any work.

Cable keeps stopping short?

Tell dispatch how far it got and which fixtures back up. That helps decide whether a camera and jet are the right first stop.
