Leaky Outdoor Faucet Repair in Winslow, NJ: Fix or Call

An outdoor faucet, also called a hose bib or spigot, leaks in one of three places: from the spout, from behind the handle, or from the pipe where it meets the house wall. The first two are often cheap repairs. A leak at the wall, or any wet spot inside the house near the spigot, can mean a cracked pipe from a freeze and needs prompt attention.

This guide walks through how to tell which leak you have, what a careful homeowner can do, and what to do before the first hard freeze in Winslow.

Fixes a careful homeowner can try

Turn off the supply to the outdoor faucet first. Some homes have a dedicated shutoff valve in the basement or crawlspace, often with a small bleeder cap. If you cannot find it, close the main shutoff and open the faucet to drain the line.

1

Disconnect the hose

A hose left on all winter is the number one reason spigots split. Remove it and any splitters before you work.

2

Tighten the packing nut

If water seeps around the handle stem, a quarter turn on the packing nut with a wrench often stops it. Do not overtighten, or the handle will bind.

3

Replace the washer

Unscrew the handle, remove the stem and swap the rubber washer at the end. Match the new one to the old in size and shape.

4

Check the seat

If the brass seat inside the body is pitted, a new washer will not seal. A plumber can replace the whole faucet.

5

Test and watch

Turn the water on, check the spout, stem and wall joint, and look at the inside wall near the pipe for stains.

Spring is the best time to test the outdoor faucet. Open it slowly with the hose off, watch the spout and the wall, and then check the room on the other side of the wall. A damp patch or a musty smell there means the pipe split over winter.

What freezing does to a spigot

Water expands as it freezes. If a standard faucet still holds water when the temperature drops, the ice can split the pipe behind the wall, even though the faucet looks fine outside. The leak then shows up in spring when the faucet is turned on, and water sprays inside the wall.

A frost-free faucet moves the shutoff deeper inside the house so the exposed part drains. It only works if it slopes slightly downward and if no hose is attached. A hose left on traps water in the barrel and defeats the design.

Do this before the first hard freeze

Remove every hose, close the interior shutoff to the outdoor faucet, open the outdoor spout to drain, and cover it with an insulated cap if the maker allows. In spring, turn it on slowly and check the inside wall.

When a leaky spigot is not just a drip

If you notice a bill higher than normal, the leak may be bigger than it looks. The Township of Winslow notes that a higher water bill can mean an undetected leak, and a spigot left dripping through a hot summer can add to it.

Check for soft ground near the foundation, water stains in a finished basement, or a damp crawlspace wall.

Cracked pipe from a winter freeze belongs with burst pipe repair. A plumber can find the break, cut out the damaged section and replace it, and can tell you if the damage reaches further into the house.

What changes the cost of a spigot repair

A washer swap is a small job. A pipe repair behind a wall is not. These items move the quote.

Type of faucetA standard spigot costs less to replace than a frost-free model with a long barrel.
AccessA wall opening in drywall, siding or brick adds labor and finish work.
Extent of damageOne split pipe is simpler than a break that has run along the wall.
Shutoff conditionA stuck or missing interior shutoff may need replacing first.
Water damageWet insulation, drywall or flooring is a separate scope.

Home policies typically pay for sudden damage, not for wear or neglect, so a freeze burst may be treated differently from a slow drip. Freeze damage claims depend on your policy, so ask your carrier before repairs and keep photos.

Winslow facts that affect your outdoor faucet

Your outdoor tap runs on Township water, which the Department of Municipal Utilities (609-567-0700) sources from ten wells in several aquifers and from New Jersey American Water. The DMU can send the annual water quality report, which has no hardness figure, so test your tap if mineral crust is eating your faucet seats.

If you live in Sicklerville (08081) or Cedar Brook (08018), check the age of your outdoor taps. Levitt-built houses in Sicklerville date from the 1970s, so spigots from the original builds may be decades old and due for a swap. If you are not sure when yours was last changed, plan to replace it rather than patch it again.

If a repair needs a permit decision, ask the Construction Office at 609-567-0700, option 5. The plumbing inspector is available Tuesday to Thursday, 8am to 1pm.
