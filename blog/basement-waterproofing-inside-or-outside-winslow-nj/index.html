Basement Waterproofing: Inside or Outside in Winslow, NJ?

Interior waterproofing collects water that gets in and sends it to a sump pump. Exterior waterproofing keeps water from reaching the wall in the first place. Neither is automatically better, and the right answer depends on where your water comes from.

Part of the job belongs to a plumber and part belongs to a waterproofing contractor. This guide shows where that line sits for a Winslow basement, so you hire the right trade for the right problem.

Who does what: plumber or waterproofing contractor

We are a plumbing dispatch service, not a waterproofing contractor. A plumber installs and repairs sump pumps, discharge lines, check valves, and the pipes that carry water out of the house. A waterproofing contractor handles excavation around the foundation, wall membranes, crack injection, and grading.

Interior waterproofing: what it is and what it costs you in space

Interior systems cut a channel along the base of the basement wall, lay drain tile or a channel drain, and route water to a sump pit. The pump then sends it out of the house. It works with the water that is already coming through the wall or floor joint, and it does not stop water from reaching the foundation.

The advantages are that the work happens indoors, it does not depend on the weather, and it can be done where the yard is blocked by a patio, deck, or neighbor's fence.

The trade-offs are a cut floor edge, some disruption inside, and a pump that must keep working. If the pump fails during a storm, water stays in the basement.

Exterior waterproofing: what it is and where it fits

Exterior work digs down along the foundation, cleans and seals the wall, and sets drainage at the footing. It aims to keep water off the wall and away from the basement. That makes it more disruptive outside, with landscaping, decks, and walkways often in the way.

Exterior work is often a fit for a wall with steady seepage, a failed existing seal, or a problem that interior drains cannot fully manage.

It is less practical where the house sits close to a property line, a driveway, or a slab. Ask a waterproofing contractor why the method they propose fits your wall, and ask for a written scope.

First, rule out the plumbing

  • ✓A running water meter with every fixture off can mean a hidden supply leak.
  • ✓A wet spot below a bathroom, laundry, or water heater often points to a pipe, not the foundation.
  • ✓A wet floor near the floor drain after heavy use points to a drain issue, not groundwater.
  • ✓Warm, damp walls near a furnace or heater suggest condensation or a leak, not seepage.
  • ✓Water that appears only after rain, along walls or the floor edge, is more likely groundwater.

If the pattern fits a pipe, leak detection can find it without opening walls. If it fits groundwater and you have a sump pit, sump pump service covers the pump, check valve, and discharge. A waterproofing contractor should look at anything that points to the foundation itself.

Winslow Township: a water table that follows the rain

NJDEP's source-water summary describes the Township's water supply as a Kirkwood-Cohansey watertable aquifer system. A watertable, or unconfined, aquifer is one whose levels follow rainfall. In practical terms, after long wet spells the ground water level can rise closer to a basement floor, and a pit that stayed dry in July can fill in March.

Practical tie-back: if your basement only gets wet after prolonged rain, note the dates and compare them to the weather. That record helps a contractor decide whether the cause is the water table, surface drainage, or a failed seal. It also helps a plumber judge whether your sump pump is sized and working for those periods.

Where the sump pump discharges matters too. EPA lists sump pumps, roof leaders, and foundation drains connected to the sanitary sewer among the connections that can overload sewers, so do not send sump water to a sanitary drain.

About 80 percent of the township lies in the Pinelands National Reserve, so ask the Township Construction Office at 609-567-0700 option 5 what an exterior dig or drainage change needs, including whether any Pinelands review applies. The office decides, and permit rules vary.

Where this leaves a Winslow homeowner

Start with the cause. Map when water appears, check for pipe leaks, and test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit until the float lifts the pump. If the pump does not start, or runs without moving water, call a plumber right away, because that is a repair and not a project.

If the water enters through the wall or floor joint, get two written proposals from waterproofing contractors. They should describe the cause they found, the method, what is included, and how the system drains.
