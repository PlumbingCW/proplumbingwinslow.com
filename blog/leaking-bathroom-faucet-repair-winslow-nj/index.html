Leaking Bathroom Faucet Repair in Winslow, NJ: How-To

Most leaking bathroom faucets in Winslow, NJ drip because of a worn internal part: a cartridge, a rubber washer, a ceramic disc or an O-ring. The fix is usually to shut off the supply under the sink, take the handle apart and replace the part that has worn out. If the faucet body is corroded or the leak is under the sink, replacement is the safer answer.

This guide helps you work out which faucet you have, which fixes are safe at home and which need a plumber.

If you cannot tell, take the handle off and photograph what you see. A plumbing supply counter can often match a cartridge from a clear photo or the old part.

Steps for a basic drip repair

1

Close the shutoff valves

Turn the small oval handles under the sink clockwise. Open the faucet to drain the line and plug the drain so no small screws are lost.

2

Remove the handle

Pry off the decorative cap, remove the screw underneath and lift off the handle. Use a cloth on the finish to avoid scratching.

3

Pull the worn part

Remove the retaining nut, then lift out the cartridge, stem or cylinder. Note the order of washers and rings.

4

Replace and reassemble

Install the matching part, coat O-rings lightly with plumber's grease, and put everything back in the reverse order.

5

Turn on slowly and check

Open the shutoffs, run the faucet and watch the base, spout and supply lines for moisture.

Stop if the shutoff valves will not close

Under-sink shutoffs that are stuck, crusty or drip when you turn them are a sign to stop. Forcing them can break the valve and flood the cabinet. Shut off the main water to the house and call a plumber.

Take your time with the reassembly, since a handle put back crooked or a nut left loose will bring the drip back by the next morning. If the new part still leaks after one careful try, stop and call a plumber instead of stacking parts on a bad seat.

Leaks that are not the faucet

A faucet that looks like it leaks can hide a different problem, and the wrong repair wastes an afternoon. Water under the sink may come from the supply line, the shutoff valve or the drain trap. Dry each connection with a paper towel and see which one gets wet first.

A wet spot on the ceiling below the bathroom, a stain on the vanity floor or a faint running sound with all fixtures off can point to a leak in the wall or floor. In that case a plumber with leak-finding tools can help, and leak detection is the right starting point rather than replacing parts at random.

Keep the old part in a bag and bring it to the supply counter. Matching a cartridge by eye is the step where most second trips begin, and a clear match on the first visit saves the afternoon.

Winslow water bills and what a drip can add

Winslow Township warns that a jump in the water bill can point to an undetected leak, with toilets and shower heads as its examples. A dripping faucet is one more, and the gallons add up over a month. If your bill jumps and the faucet drips, fix the drip and then check the toilets.

For help reading your account or questions about water service, call the Township's Department of Municipal Utilities at 609-567-0700, weekdays 8:30 to 4:30. The Township's water report does not list a hardness figure, so if white crust forms on your fixtures, test your tap and ask the DMU for the annual report.

A faucet replacement rarely raises big permit questions, but the Construction Office at 609-567-0700, option 5, has the final word. Licensed work in the state follows the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated.
