How to Install a Garbage Disposal in Winslow, NJ Homes

Installing a garbage disposal means mounting a grinder under the sink flange, linking it to the drain trap and, if you have one, the dishwasher, and powering it safely. A handy homeowner can do a straight swap of one disposal for another in an afternoon. A first-time install with new wiring or drain changes is a job for a licensed plumber and, for new circuits, a licensed electrician.

Below is the safe version of the process, the points where most DIY jobs go wrong, and a clear list of when to stop and call.

Check these three things before you buy a unit

First, confirm you can reach power. Many sinks have a switched outlet under the cabinet, and some have a hardwired box. If neither is there, the install needs electrical work before any plumbing starts.

Second, look at your drain. A double-bowl sink with one trap, and a dishwasher hose that already connects to the old unit are signs of a simple swap. Third, find out whether the house is on public sewer or a septic tank, because a disposal sends more solids into either system.

The installation steps, in plain order

1

Kill the power

Turn off the breaker and unplug the old unit or confirm wires are dead with a tester. Never trust a wall switch alone, because someone can turn it on while your hands are underneath.

2

Remove the old unit

Support the disposal, release the mounting ring and lower it. Disconnect the drain trap and the dishwasher hose, with a bucket ready for water.

3

Set the new mount

Clean the sink flange, add plumber's putty or the supplied gasket as the maker directs, and tighten the mounting ring so the flange seats evenly.

4

Knock out the dishwasher plug

On a new unit that serves a dishwasher, the plug in the inlet must be knocked out from inside before the hose goes on. Forgetting this step is a common cause of a dishwasher that will not drain.

5

Connect, power and test

Attach the discharge elbow, rebuild the trap, wire or plug in the unit, and run water for a minute. Check every joint with a dry paper towel before closing the cabinet.

Where DIY jobs go wrong

Overtightening plastic parts is the top problem. A cracked drain elbow or a warped gasket may hold for a day and then drip into the cabinet base for weeks.

The second problem is the trap arrangement. If the disposal outlet sits lower than the trap or the line slopes the wrong way, the sink drains slowly and food scraps sit in the pipe. That is a layout issue, and tightening more will not cure it.

The third is an electrical shortcut. Do not splice into an unmarked cable, use a damaged cord or wire a disposal to a circuit you have not checked. If you are unsure, an electrician should make the connection and our team can handle the drain side.

Stop and call if you see this

Water rising from the cabinet floor, a smell of burning near the motor, rust-stained pipes that crumble when touched, or wiring you cannot identify. Turn off the breaker and the sink supply valves and call a licensed professional.

Sewer, septic and permits in Winslow Township

Not every Winslow street is on public sewer. The Township's Department of Municipal Utilities (609-567-0700, weekdays 8:30 to 4:30) takes sewer connection applications, and Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority approves a separate application. If your home is on septic, a disposal adds fine solids to the tank, so ask your septic contractor before you buy.

For sewer hookups the Township Construction Department issues the permit, a registered plumber does the work and the plumbing inspector must approve it. A simple under-sink swap is a different scale, and permit rules vary, so ask the Construction Office at 609-567-0700, option 5. The plumbing inspector works Tuesday to Thursday mornings, 8am to 1pm.

Under the state plumbing subcode, the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, a master plumber or authorized plumbing contractor must do licensed work. Licensees must display their license number on vehicles and advertising, so ask to see it.

What a plumber adds that a kit does not

A plumber checks the trap, the vent and the downstream pipe before the new unit goes in. A disposal can only work as well as the drain it feeds, so a slow line should be cleared first. If your kitchen drain already backs up, drain cleaning ahead of the install saves a second visit.

If you have a unit that hums, leaks or jams, repair may cost less than replacement. Our garbage disposal repair service starts with an inspection to see whether a reset, a new gasket or a new unit is the honest answer.
