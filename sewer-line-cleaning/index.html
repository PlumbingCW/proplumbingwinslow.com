What makes a main sewer line stop

Most main line blockages come from the same short list. Grease and wipes collect on the pipe wall, roots work into joints, and the pipe can sag where soil has shifted. Each one leaves a different mark when we clear it, which is why we pay attention to what comes back on the cable.

Grease and scale

Builds up slowly, mostly on the bottom of the pipe, until flow narrows.

Wipes and paper products

Labelled flushable does not mean the line can carry them. They catch on rough spots and knit together.

Roots

Fine roots enter at joints and then thicken. They catch everything that passes.

Belly or offset

A low spot or shifted joint holds water and solids. It will clog again until repaired.

Cleaning removes the blockage. It does not remove the cause, so if the line is sagging or cracked, we say so rather than letting you pay for the same visit over and over.

Sewer backup warning signs inside the house

  • ✓Gurgling in a toilet or tub when another fixture drains
  • ✓Water rising in the lowest drain in the house first
  • ✓A sewer odor in the basement or crawlspace
  • ✓Several fixtures slow or full at the same time
  • ✓Wet spots near a cleanout cap

The lowest drain in the house shows a backup first, because water takes the easiest path. That is usually a basement floor drain or a ground-floor shower. If you see this, a same-day appointment when available is the right call, not a wait-and-see.

Township sewer or septic, and why it matters here

Not every Winslow street is on public sewer. The Township process for a sewer connection involves the Department of Municipal Utilities, an approval from the Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority, a permit from the Township Construction Department and a plumbing inspection. Homes without that connection use septic. Ask the DMU at 609-567-0700 which applies to your street.

If you are on septic, a backup can mean the tank is full or the field is failing, and that is a septic contractor's job.
